Island ecosystems are among the most vulnerable to biodiversity loss and environmental disruption, yet they often lack the long-term data needed to understand ecological change. Invasive mammals degrade native vegetation and drive species extinction, reducing ecosystem stability and leaving islands more exposed to drought, erosion, and extreme weather. While removing these species is a proven strategy, tracking ecological recovery over time, and knowing where to focus next, remains a major challenge.

The tool is a NASA-powered decision-support tool that helps conservation teams monitor vegetation recovery and ecosystem resilience after restoration actions. Built on a globally consistent, scalable framework, it uses 40 years of satellite data to detect changes in canopy cover, vegetation health, and moisture retention across more than 50 islands.

The tool provides detailed, location-specific insights for each island, delivering value to conservation decision-makers and practitioners by enabling:

1.  Remote assessments of ecological change in data limited or hard-to-access areas.

2.  Prioritization of restoration, rewilding, and monitoring efforts based on where change is happening.

3.  Reporting on ecosystem outcomes tied to vegetation stability, drought resilience, and carbon storage.

### **Core modules:**

#### Per-Pixel Annual Vegetation

Tracks yearly modeled values of canopy cover, moisture, and productivity from 1984 to today, at up to 30-meter resolution, enabling users to see how specific parts of an island are changing over time.

#### Change Maps

Visualizes where vegetation has improved or declined before and after conservation actions, helping identify where restoration is succeeding, where nature is recovering on its own, and where more action is needed.

### **Key Terms:**

#### Vegetation Metrics

**Canopy cover (or Tree Canopy Cover, "TCC"):** Shows forest regrowth and tracks ecosystem stability as vegetation returns. It is the global standard for measuring forest restoration success and indicates resilient ecosystems and carbon storage.

**Canopy Moisture ("NDMI"):** Measures vegetation water content and recovery patterns to identify drought resilience, fire risk, and climate refugia. It is widely used by climate researchers.

**Productivity ("NDVI," "EVI," or "MSAVI2"):** Captures plant growth and ecosystem function to monitor vegetation health and recovery. It is a gold standard used in thousands of restoration studies.

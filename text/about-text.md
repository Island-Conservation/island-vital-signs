Island Vital Signs is a tool that uses data collected from satellites to visualize ecosystem change on islands around the world. Conservation teams have used it to identify areas where island vegetation communities have changed over time. It has also been used to guide planning for current and future restoration actions. 

### **Methods & Data Products**

Here, we provide an overview of the methods used to create Island Vital Signs. For more information on our modeling framework, please view the methods described by Honzák et al. (2024) in Environmental Research Letters or the pre-print from Wails et al. (2026) available at: https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-10554051/v1.

We use satellite data from the NASA Landsat program to track changes in island vegetation structure and condition through time. From these images, we measure three vegetation metrics that provide insight into ecosystem structure and function: tree canopy cover (“TCC”), canopy moisture (“NDMI”), and productivity (“NDVI”, “EVI”, or “MSAVI2”).
